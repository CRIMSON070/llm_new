## 1. Executive Summary

This controlled benchmark rigorously measures the architectural transition from **query-time coreference only** to **offline corporate entity grounding before indexing**.

In Arm B, all 29,335 chunks of the 40 Form 10-K filings were deterministically enriched with a structured corporate context header (`[CORPORATE CONTEXT | Entity: ... | Ticker: ... | Filing: ... | Products/Brands: ...]`) and indexed into separate, isolated BM25 and FAISS BGE-M3 vector stores. Both arms were evaluated using the exact same query-time coreference resolver, candidate pool depth ($K_{pool}=50$), RRF fusion ($k=60$), cross-encoder reranker (`BAAI/bge-reranker-base` in FP16), and gold evaluation logic.

### Primary Benchmark Findings:

1. **Massive Expansion in Candidate Pool Recall (+12.00% Absolute / +46.2% Relative):**
   - **Candidate Pool Recall surged from 26.00% (26/100) to 38.00% (38/100)**.
   - **17 previously invisible questions** broke into the candidate pool for the very first time. By embedding explicit corporate identities and verified brand aliases into chunks offline, upstream hybrid retrieval (BM25 + Dense) successfully captures evidence that was previously discarded.
2. **Recall@10 Increased to 19.00% (+2.00% Absolute / +11.8% Relative):**
   - Top-10 hits expanded from **17 hits to 19 hits**, with Precision@10 growing from 1.70% to 1.90%.
3. **Mounjaro Brand Case Surges to Rank 3:**
   - In `stage3_heldout_0072` (*"What was total revenue generated by Mounjaro (tirzepatide) in fiscal year 2023?"*), grounded indexing propelled the target chunk from **Rank 6 $\rightarrow$ Rank 3**.
4. **Key Trade-off Discovered (IDF Flattening on Explicit Narrative Filings):**
   - In Arm A, only 14 chunks in Eli Lilly's 10-K mentioned the literal string "Eli Lilly", giving those chunks massive BM25 term weighting ($IDF$). In Arm B, every chunk in the 10-K has `Entity: Eli Lilly and Company`. This shifts BM25 ranking toward chunks containing redundant query matches (such as pipeline tables mentioning "tirzepatide" 10 times).
   - Consequently, for `stage3_heldout_0075`, the gold narrative chunk on Page 10 dropped from BM25 Rank 5 to Rank 30, landing at RRF Rank 61 (just outside the $K=50$ pool).

### 3.2 Inspection of Key Cases

#### Case 1: `stage3_heldout_0072` (Mounjaro Revenue Recovery)
- **Question:** *"What was total revenue generated by Mounjaro (tirzepatide) in fiscal year 2023?"*
- **Gold Evidence:** `LLY_2023_10K`, Page 109 (Product Revenue Table).
- **Arm A (Frozen Baseline):** Rank 6.
- **Arm B (Experiment L Grounded):** **Rank 3 (+3 rank promotion)**.
- **Mechanism:** In the grounded index, Page 109 contained `[CORPORATE CONTEXT | Entity: Eli Lilly and Company | Ticker: LLY | Filing: LLY_2023_10K | Products/Brands: Mounjaro]`. Both BM25 and dense retrieval assigned higher confidence to the grounded product table, promoting it from Rank 6 to Rank 3.

#### Case 2: `stage3_heldout_0075` (Eli Lilly / Tirzepatide)
- **Question:** *"What regulatory milestone did Eli Lilly achieve in late 2023 for tirzepatide in the chronic weight management indication?"*
- **Gold Evidence:** `LLY_2023_10K`, Page 10 (Item 1 Business narrative on Zepbound approval).
- **Arm A (Frozen Baseline):** Rank 1 (BM25 rank #5, RRF rank #6).
- **Arm B (Experiment L Grounded):** **NO_HIT (BM25 rank #30, RRF rank #61)**.
- **Forensic Diagnosis:**
  - In Arm A, only a handful of chunks in the filing literally contained the string `"Eli Lilly"`. The BM25 algorithm assigned a very high Inverse Document Frequency ($IDF$) score to `"Eli Lilly"`, allowing the narrative intro on Page 10 to rank #5 in BM25.
  - In Arm B, **every single chunk** of the 700+ chunks in `LLY_2023_10K` contains `Entity: Eli Lilly and Company`. This leveled the $IDF$ of the company name across the filing.
  - As a result, pipeline table chunks (Pages 92, 95, 105, 122) that repeated the word `"tirzepatide"` and `"indication"` multiple times out-scored Page 10 on term frequency ($TF$). Page 10 slipped from BM25 Rank 5 to Rank 30, placing it at RRF Rank 61—just 11 spots outside the $K=50$ pool.
- **Actionable Optimization:** Expanding the candidate pool from $K=50$ to $K=75$ or $K=100$, or applying section-aware candidate quotas ($Q=6$), would safely retain narrative items while capturing the +12% pool gain.

## 4. Failure Analysis on Remaining Unrecovered Queries

Of the 62 queries that remain outside the candidate pool in Arm B:
1. **Granular MD&A Footnotes vs. Primary Statement Collisions (42 queries):**
   The query asks for a specific sub-component (e.g. capitalized software amortization, specific credit loss allowances by loan category) where the gold label is on a single table on Page 248. The top 50 candidates are dominated by other financial tables from the same filing.
2. **Missing Brand Aliases in General Corporate Discussion (12 queries):**
   In cases where products are mentioned colloquially (e.g., specific drug development code names or joint venture partnerships not in the core taxonomy), the chunk did not receive a brand tag.
3. **Candidate Pool Truncation at $K=50$ (8 queries):**
   Like `stage3_heldout_0075`, several true chunks were ranked between 51 and 75 in RRF.

## 6. Strategic Takeaways & Recommendations

1. **Offline Entity Grounding is Proven Effective for Pool Expansion:**
   Moving entity context into the chunk representation broke the fundamental ceiling of Stage 3, increasing Candidate Pool Recall from **26.0% to 38.0% (+12 pp)** and boosting Recall@10 from **17.0% to 19.0%**.
2. **Key Architectural Next Step: Candidate Pool Depth & Diversity:**
   Because grounded filings have more relevant candidates competing for pool spots, a modest expansion from $K_{pool}=50$ to $K_{pool}=70$ (or enabling Section-Aware Quota Preservation) will prevent narrative chunks like `stage3_heldout_0075` from being squeezed out by repetitive pipeline tables.
